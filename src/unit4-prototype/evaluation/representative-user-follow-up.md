# Representative User Follow-Up

## Method

On October 19, 2026, follow-up feedback was obtained from three employees who had participated in earlier prototype testing: a receptionist representing a nontechnical end user, a Level 1 Help Desk technician, and a systems administrator. Their identities were anonymized, and the prototype used synthetic information rather than personal or production data. The employees formed a small internal convenience sample. This was a qualitative follow-up to the earlier testing, not a new controlled usability study. No new numerical usability score was calculated.

## Feedback

| Participant | User profile | Follow-up feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participant A | Receptionist and nontechnical end user | The general workflow was understandable, but technical terms such as identity access and endpoint compliance required additional explanation. After receiving a plain-language explanation, the participant better understood the decisions and recommended actions. |
| Participant B | Level 1 Help Desk technician | The workflow was clear, and the explanations helped communicate why an access or compliance decision was produced. |
| Participant C | Systems administrator | The decision results and supporting explanations were useful for reviewing access and device-compliance conditions. |

## Resulting Change

The interface now defines identity access, multifactor authentication, endpoint, device compliance, and remediation in plain language. It also adds a plain-language sentence beneath each technical decision, gives a specific corrective action for each failed device-security condition, and visibly states that the decision log is cleared when the page is refreshed or closed.

The follow-up expands the earlier single-reviewer usability evidence, but it does not replace structured task-based testing with a larger participant group. Future evaluation should record task completion, time, assistance, errors, and participant ratings under a consistent protocol.
